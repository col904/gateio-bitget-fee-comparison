## The VIP ladder is where the two stop looking alike

Gate runs VIP 0 through VIP 16. Bitget's tiers run up to VIP 7 by Gate Research's own comparison table. A finer ladder is not automatically better, but it does mean smaller jumps between levels, and the jump from VIP 4 to VIP 5 on Gate moves your taker rate from 0.096% to 0.095%, which is the kind of detail that matters to someone running size.

Qualification is where Gate gets more complicated than most. Your tier is set by the best of three tracks, recalculated automatically:

1. **30-day weighted trading volume.** Spot and stock volume count at 100%. USDT and BTC perpetuals and delivery futures count at 40%. USD1 futures and options count at 20%. CFDs count at 10%.
2. **14-day average GT holdings**, including GT2.
3. **Account asset value.**

That weighting is why a futures-heavy account and a spot-heavy account with identical headline turnover can land on different tiers. Gate's own fee guide describes automatic tier updates and a protection window before a downgrade takes effect after your volume drops, which is the reason you can have a quiet month without your rate snapping back immediately.

## Products that only exist on one side

Gate's breadth is the argument its users make most often. CoinGecko's exchange pages, checked in September 2026, show Gate with roughly 1,687 coins and 2,205 spot pairs against Bitget's 580 coins and 1,212 spot pairs. Gate also carries around 671 perpetual pairs, plus European-style options, gold and stock CFDs, and event contracts. Bitget doesn't offer options.

Then there's stock exposure, and the two platforms mean different things by it.

- **Gate Stocks** covers more than 12,500 stocks and ETFs across US, Hong Kong, Korean and Japanese markets inside one account, with fractional shares and corporate-action support. Trading in eligible US stocks and ETFs carries zero commission, zero account-opening fee and zero maintenance fee, though third-party settlement and regulatory charges still apply.
- **Bitget** offers stock futures on tokenised names such as TSLAUSDT and NVDAUSDT with leverage up to 25x. That's a derivative on a stock, not the stock. If you want to hold the share, Gate's product is the one that does that.

On the other side of the ledger, Bitget's copy trading is the most developed version of the product among major venues, by its own account and by third-party write-ups. Following a trader costs you a profit-share fee, typically 10% to 20% of realised profit, set by the trader, charged only when a copied position closes in profit, plus the normal trading fees and funding. Gate offers copy trading too, but it's a secondary product there rather than the centrepiece.

Both run trading bots. On Bitget, self-configured bots including spot grid, futures grid and spot-futures grid carry no separate subscription, so you pay standard trading fees and nothing else. P2P and Convert are zero-fee on Bitget, and the platform states there are no deposit fees and no custody or maintenance charges.

## What the listing numbers don't tell you

Listing count is a vanity metric until you look at what happens afterward. A CoinMarketCap study covering 1 January 2023 to 31 December 2024 found that of the tokens listed on seven major exchanges, Gate listed 1,186 tokens and delisted 4.64% of them, while Bitget listed 901 and delisted 43.17%. Gate had the most listings in the set; Bitget had by far the highest delisting rate.

The study is careful to note that neither number is automatically good or bad. A low delisting rate can reflect thorough upfront diligence, or slow housekeeping. A high one can signal aggressive listing that creates churn. What's not in dispute is the practical consequence: if you buy long-tail assets on a venue with high turnover, the tail you bought may not be listed there in eighteen months. The same study also found Gate's listings were weighted toward tokens hitting all-time highs over short windows and less toward price longevity, which fits how the platform gets used in practice.

## Regulation, reserves and who can actually open an account

Gate holds MiCA authorisation through Malta's MFSA, an FSA registration in Japan through Gate Japan K.K., a Dubai VARA licence and AUSTRAC registration in Australia. It publishes a reserve ratio above 100%, with figures disclosed during 2026 sitting in the 115% to 122% range depending on the month, backed by zero-knowledge proofs and a Merkle tree. In a July 2026 account-security dispute, the company committed to full compensation for losses attributable to its own systems.

Bitget publishes monthly proof of reserves, describes a protection fund of more than $300 million, and holds a MiCA licence for its EU entity. It rolled back futures trades during the April 2025 VOXEL incident and pledged compensation, a decision that was controversial even among people who thought it was the right call.

Availability is a harder constraint than fees for a lot of readers:

- Neither global platform serves US residents. Gate has a separate US entity, and third-party trackers list a very short roster of assets there.
- Neither is open to UK retail customers.
- Both serve the EEA through MiCA-authorized entities.
- Japan is where they diverge sharply. Gate serves it through its registered local entity. Bitget announced that service to Japan residents moves to close-only from 1 November 2026 and ends on 31 December 2026, so if you're in Japan and reading a comparison written before that announcement, Bitget is off the table.

## The costs that never make it into a fee table

Trading fees are the visible part of the bill. These are the ones people discover later.

**Withdrawals.** Both charge no deposit fee and pass only network costs, but withdrawal pricing is where third-party comparisons disagree wildly. For BTC, 2026 write-ups reported anything from 0.0001 BTC to 0.001 BTC depending on the platform and the date the page was checked. Treat every published withdrawal figure as stale and read the confirmation screen instead, since it shows the exact amount before you approve.

**Funding intervals.** Bitget settles perpetual funding every 8 hours, as its support pages state. On Gate, third-party trackers report a 4-hour settlement interval on some contracts while Gate's own guide describes the standard 8-hour cycle. If you hold a position for days rather than hours, check the funding interval on the specific contract panel, because that single setting can outweigh the difference in taker fees.

**Everything outside spot and futures.** Gate's Alpha market charges a flat 0.8% at every VIP level, with no tier discount. Margin interest is asset-specific on both. Copy-trading profit share on Bitget sits entirely outside the published fee schedule. Bitget Card charges 0.9% per transaction, while Gate's own research lists card cashback of up to 2.5% at its top VIP tier.

## Getting the rate you think you signed up for

The gap between the advertised entry rate and the rate that actually bills you is where most fee complaints come from. Four steps cover most of it.

1. **Register through a link that carries the referral relationship, then finish verification.** Both platforms require KYC for core functions, and an unverified account won't trade.
2. **Turn on the token discount manually.** Neither GT deduction on Gate nor BGB offset on Bitget switches itself on. On Bitget, if your BGB balance can't cover the fee, the charge silently reverts to the standard method.
3. **Check your actual tier in the account fee page**, not on the marketing page. Your tier comes from the highest of volume, token holdings or asset value, so it can be higher than your volume alone suggests.
4. **Recheck after a quiet month.** Gate recalculates automatically, and the weights on futures volume mean a month of heavy perp trading counts for less than it feels like it should.

## FAQ

**Is Gate or Bitget cheaper overall?**
On base spot rates they're tied at 0.10%. Bitget edges ahead on spot once BGB is applied, 0.08% against Gate's 0.09% with GT. On perpetual taker fees Gate is cheaper at 0.050% against 0.060%, and that gap widens with size because Bitget's token discount doesn't apply to futures.

**Which has more coins?**
Gate, by a wide margin: roughly 1,687 coins and 2,205 spot pairs per CoinGecko's September 2026 data, against 580 coins and 1,212 pairs on Bitget. Gate's delisting rate was also far lower over 2023–2024, though the study behind that figure notes a low rate can reflect either diligence or slow housekeeping.

**Can I use either one if I live in the US or UK?**
No. Neither global platform serves US residents, and neither is open to UK retail customers. EEA users are served through MiCA-authorized entities on both.

**Which is better for a beginner?**
If by beginner you mean someone who wants to mirror experienced traders rather than pick entries, Bitget's copy-trading ecosystem is the more developed product, at a cost of 10% to 20% of realised profit to the trader you follow. If you want access to a very long list of tokens, Gate lists more, with the corresponding caveat that long-tail assets are volatile and sometimes delisted.
